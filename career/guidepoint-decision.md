# GuidePoint (SoCal, Commercial AE) vs staying at Cato

A decision framework and diligence checklist. Fill in the unknowns before deciding anything.

---

## The real question

This is not "new job or old job." It is a fork between two career tracks:

- **Vendor track:** you sell one platform deeply. Cato SDR to Cato AE to enterprise AE to leadership. Deep product mastery, strong enablement, one story.
- **Channel / solutions provider track:** you sell dozens of vendors' products and services. Breadth over depth, consultative by necessity, you learn the whole security market fast.

Both are legitimate. Many strong vendor AEs came up through the channel, and vendors actively recruit from their partners because those people already know the ecosystem and the buyers. GuidePoint almost certainly resells Cato, which means the Cato knowledge you are building does not get thrown away. It becomes one of the products you carry.

## The hardest jump in sales is SDR to AE

Not AE to senior AE. Not company to company. The first carry-a-quota seat is the bottleneck, because everyone wants proven closers and nobody wants to be the one who takes the risk. Once you have 12 to 18 months of AE numbers behind you, the market opens up dramatically and you can move almost anywhere, including back to a vendor at a better level than you would get promoting internally today.

That fact should weigh heavily. An AE title in hand beats an AE title promised.

## The case FOR pursuing GuidePoint

- Title and quota now instead of a promise with no date
- Breadth across the whole security stack, which attacks the "I am not technical" gap from a different angle than deep Cato study does
- You learn how buyers actually evaluate and buy security, across vendors, which makes you better at any vendor job later
- Channel experience is directly relevant, and you just lived it with the Clutch and Pacific Rim situation
- SoCal territory, security market, strong brand in the space
- Even if you stay, going through the process gives you market information about your own value

## The case AGAINST

- You have real momentum at Cato: manager relationship, partner relationships, a documented plan, and an evidence file building
- "Commercial AE" at a solutions provider can mean high-velocity quoting and renewals rather than net-new hunting. Those are very different reps, and the wrong kind is a bad first AE job
- Comp structures at resellers are often margin-based rather than straight quota on ARR. Different math, sometimes lower, needs real scrutiny
- Less enablement and marketing support than a funded vendor. More self-reliance, which can be great or can sink a first-time AE
- If you are early in your SDR tenure, the leap may be premature and a bad first AE year is genuinely costly

## Diligence questions (ask all of these before an offer means anything)

**About the role itself**
1. Is this net-new hunting, existing account management, or renewals and quoting? What is the actual mix in percentages?
2. What does the current team's attainment look like? What percentage hit quota last year?
3. Who was the last person in this seat and what happened to them?
4. Is it a new seat or a backfill?
5. What does the first 90 days look like? Do I inherit accounts or start from zero?

**About support**
6. Do I have dedicated SE support? What is the ratio?
7. Is there an SDR or BDR supporting this patch, or am I self-sourcing everything?
8. What does ramp look like, and is there a ramp quota or draw?
9. What does enablement look like for someone coming from a single-vendor background?

**About the money**
10. Base, OTE, and how is commission actually calculated? Margin-based or revenue-based?
11. What did top performers earn last year? What did median earn?
12. Accelerators, caps, clawbacks, and how are renewals compensated versus net new?

**About the territory**
13. What is the patch, by geography and segment? How many named accounts?
14. Is the territory greenfield or already worked?

**About the company**
15. How does GuidePoint win against other solutions providers in SoCal?
16. What is the vendor mix, and where does Cato sit in it?

## The parallel play at Cato

Pursuing this does not require telling Cato anything, and you should not use an offer as a threat. That reads as disloyal and it poisons the relationship even when it works.

What you should do regardless:
- Go back to your manager with the criteria question again: "what specifically do I need to show, and what is the realistic timeline for a commercial seat?" Ask for specifics and a date.
- If a real offer materializes, you can be honest without weaponizing it: "I was approached and I took the conversation seriously because I want an AE seat. I would rather earn one here. Can we talk about what that path actually looks like?"
- That conversation is legitimate and most good managers respect it. A manager who punishes it has told you something useful.

## How to decide

Take the GuidePoint role if: it is genuine net-new hunting, comp is competitive, there is SE support, AND Cato cannot give you a seat with a real date.

Stay at Cato if: the role turns out to be transactional or renewal-heavy, OR Cato commits to a specific seat and timeline, OR you are early enough in your SDR tenure that another two or three quarters of overperformance makes you a strong internal candidate.

Do not decide on title alone. A bad first AE year is harder to recover from than six more months as a top SDR.
