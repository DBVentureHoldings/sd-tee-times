# GuidePoint Security: company research and interview prep

Research compiled 2026-09-30. Figures come from third-party databases and vary by source, so treat them as directional and verify anything decision-critical in conversation.

---

## The company

- Founded 2011 by Michael Volk and Justin Morehouse. Headquarters in the Reston / Herndon, Virginia area; a new global HQ opened recently.
- Roughly 1,000 to 1,150 employees depending on source and date.
- Revenue reported between roughly $194M and $252M depending on source. Reseller revenue definitions vary wildly (gross billings versus net revenue), so this range is less meaningful than it looks. Ask how they count it.
- Three consecutive years of double-digit revenue growth and three new offices opened in 2025. Inc. 5000 honoree.
- Positioning: vendor-objective security solutions provider with 800+ vendor relationships. Services span assessments, MDR, incident response, compliance, penetration testing, staff augmentation, virtual CISO, and technology implementation, alongside product resale.

## Ownership

- Private, private-equity backed. Audax Private Equity led a growth round that closed October 2023; ABS Capital Partners was an earlier investor and participated. Terms undisclosed.
- Implication: typical PE hold periods run three to seven years, so a liquidity event, recapitalization, or new sponsor is plausible within the next couple of years. That can mean upside if employees hold equity, or disruption if strategy shifts.

## Structure: "franchise-ish" is the actual term

GuidePoint runs a **Regional Partner model**, described in trade press as "franchise-ish." It is not a franchise in the legal sense (nobody buys a franchise license), but the operating logic is similar:

- Regions own their own go-to-market strategy and approach, plus some technical delivery.
- Centralized functions include information assurance, managed services, and back office.
- The pitch is "never more than one step away from an actual owner," meaning local ownership with national scale and purchasing power.
- Named regional partners have included Chris York (Northeast), Ernest Dunn (Mid-Atlantic), Brian Brush (North Central), and Matt Peterson and Jerry O'Brien (Heartland).

**The career signal in this structure is significant.** In July 2026 GuidePoint appointed Scott Rachford as CEO. Rachford spent more than a decade at the company, most recently as Regional Partner for North Central, and joined originally from Qualys where he was VP of North American Field Operations. Founder Michael Volk moved to Executive Chairman. A regional sales leader became CEO. That is a concrete, recent demonstration that the field path leads to the top of this company.

## Southern California

- Office in Irvine, CA (43 Discovery, Suite 210).
- The West and Southwest are comparatively newer territories. GuidePoint announced expansion into Arizona, California, Nevada, Utah and Hawaii with Martin Henderson hired as SVP of the Southwest, plus a separate expansion into Northern California, Washington, Oregon, Idaho, Montana and Wyoming.
- **Implication:** SoCal is closer to greenfield than to a mature, fully-worked patch. Good for a hunter, since there is room. Harder in that local brand recognition is thinner than on the East Coast and you will be doing more education.

## Sales role data (RepVue and Glassdoor)

This is the most decision-relevant data found.

- RepVue score 87.11, top 10% of all companies rated. Ranked #200 of 6,988 for culture and leadership at 4.5 out of 5.
- Account Executive median base salary roughly $100,000, median on-target earnings roughly $250,000.
- Base-to-variable split approximately 35/65.
- **Roughly 69% of Account Executives hit or exceed quota.** Industry norms are frequently in the 40 to 50 percent range, so this is a strong number.
- Top reported total compensation in a year: about $1.67M.
- Glassdoor: compensation and benefits 4.5, work-life balance 4.3, culture and values 4.5, career opportunities 4.1.

**How to read this:** the attainment rate is the standout and directly reduces the biggest risk of a first AE seat, which is joining a team where nobody hits number. The 35/65 split is the counterweight: with a $100K base and $250K OTE, roughly $150K is at risk. That is the number to interrogate given a new family.

## Market and opportunity

- The overall cybersecurity market is estimated somewhere around $212B to $277B in 2026 depending on source, growing at roughly 11 to 13 percent annually.
- The cybersecurity **services** segment is growing faster than the market overall, with one estimate putting it near 15 percent CAGR.
- Services growing faster than product favors GuidePoint's model, since services carry higher margin and create stickier customer relationships than resale alone.

## Competitive landscape

- Optiv is the closest direct competitor and is larger by headcount.
- Others in the mix: Trace3, Presidio, Kudelski Security, Critical Start, Novacoast, plus broadline players like CDW, Insight, WWT and ePlus that compete on scale rather than security specialization.
- GuidePoint's differentiation claim is security-only focus plus vendor objectivity, versus broadline resellers who sell everything and versus vendors who sell one thing.

## Questions to ask them

**On the role**
1. Walk me through the last three deals this team closed. Who sourced them and how?
2. What percentage of bookings in this segment came from net new logos versus existing accounts last year?
3. How much pipeline comes from vendor field reps bringing deals to GuidePoint versus self-sourced?

**On the money (most important given a new family)**
4. What is the base, and is there a ramp draw? How long does it run and is it recoverable?
5. Is commission calculated on gross margin or revenue? How are services versus product compensated?
6. RepVue shows about 69% attainment company-wide. What was it for this specific region last year?

**On the territory**
7. The West looks like a newer region. Is this patch greenfield, and what does existing penetration look like in SoCal?
8. What is the realistic travel expectation? Mostly regional driving, or flying?

**On the structure and future**
9. How does the Regional Partner model work in practice for an AE? What is the path from AE toward regional leadership?
10. Do AEs participate in equity? With Audax invested since 2023, how should I think about a future liquidity event?
11. Your new CEO came up through the Regional Partner role. How common is that internal path?

## The SoCal competitive landscape (added 2026-10-01)

The three firms a SoCal buyer might realistically compare.

| | GuidePoint | EVOTEK | Bird Rock Systems |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founded | 2011 | 2014 | 2003 |
| HQ | Reston, VA (Irvine office) | San Diego | San Diego (+ Aliso Viejo, LA) |
| Headcount | ~1,000 to 1,150 | ~255 (sources vary, some say 51-200) | ~65 (sources vary up to 200) |
| Reported revenue | ~$194M to $252M | ~$53M to $250M depending on source | ~$16.3M (2025) |
| Focus | Security only | Security plus cloud, data center, mobility, digital | Security, network, cloud, privacy, compliance |
| Ownership | PE-backed (Audax, ABS Capital) | Appears founder/management led, verify | Founder-led (James Matteo, CEO) |
| Reach | National, regional partner model | San Diego base, broader reach | Southern California regional |

**Important caveat on revenue:** solution providers report inconsistently. Some publish gross billings including product pass-through, others publish net revenue (their margin plus services). A firm showing $16M net could be moving far more in gross product volume. Never compare these numbers across firms without asking how each counts.

### What actually separates them

**Specialization.** GuidePoint is security only and sells primarily to the CISO. EVOTEK spans security plus infrastructure, cloud and digital, so it also sells to the CIO. Bird Rock is broad IT with a security emphasis. Narrower focus means a deeper specialist bench; broader focus means more ways into an account.

**Bench depth, which is what a seller actually sells.** At roughly a thousand people, GuidePoint can put incident responders, penetration testers, compliance specialists and a virtual CISO in front of a customer. At sixty-five people, the bench is thinner, so the mix tilts toward product resale and vendor-delivered services. As a rep, bench depth determines how large and how complex a deal you can credibly chase.

**Ownership and what it does to culture.** PE backing brings growth targets, process, structured enablement and a possible liquidity event, along with the pressure and the chance of a sponsor change. Founder-led firms tend to be more personal and flexible with a lower ceiling and no exit event. Neither is better; they are different bets.

**Local incumbency versus national scale.** In SoCal, EVOTEK and Bird Rock are the hometown incumbents with long relationships. GuidePoint is the national specialist entering a newer territory with more purchasing power and a deeper bench. That is exactly the competitive dynamic a GuidePoint rep in SoCal would be selling against.

**Geography matters personally.** GuidePoint's SoCal office is in Irvine. EVOTEK and Bird Rock are headquartered in San Diego. For someone living in San Diego with a young family, that difference is real even in a mostly remote role.
